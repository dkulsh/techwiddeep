We techies often overlook our clothes and dressing sense. This blog aims to highlight this habit and encourage a change.

Some points I mention may sound prejudicial, partly because they relate to cultural differences.

#### My Journey from clueless to less clueless

Growing up in a small North Indian town, I never realized how much "looks" mattered. Sure, I saw fancy-looking folks, but their extra effort always seemed unnecessary. **Seemed like a waste of time to impress people who didn’t matter.**  ![North Indian style icon](/images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/north-indian-style-icon.png)

Then I moved to South India, where I encountered simpler styles. South Indians lead modest lives, reflected in their straightforward dressing.

Even as a simply dressed North Indian, the South Indian approach was a shock. I had seen dull colors before, but never such disregard for combinations, accessories, and overall appearance.
But still respected cultural differences and accepted them as they were.

> A happy side-effect: my underdressed North Indian vibe suddenly made me the *overdressed* guy in South India!

![Cultural dressing differences](/images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/cultural-dressing-differences.jpg)

Later, I moved to the US for work, facing another surprise. Westerners are conscious of their presentation; likely a cultural trait.

From crisp ironed collared shirts to polished shoes, and clean-shaven faces to trimmed beards, they looked sharp. Europeans took it a step further.
![European style icon](/images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/european-style-icon.png)

Most appeared presentable.

That US stint flipped my perspective. **Dressing well isn’t just cultural—it’s a statement.** It’s not always about vanity; it’s also about respect—for yourself and others. **Have you ever felt underdressed next to someone who’s got it together?** ![European style icon](/images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/european-style-icon-2.png)


#### Why Bother?

If a picture’s worth a thousand words, let’s save a few thousand with some visuals. Pause and check these out:

![](images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/product-packaging-1.jpg) ![](images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/product-packaging-2.jpg)

![](images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/product-packaging-3.jpg) ![](images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/product-packaging-comparison.jpg)

Ask yourself:
- **How were the color combos?**
- **Did the packaging make the product more usable?**
- **Did it make you *want* the product more?**
- **Could the better-packaged one sell for more?**

Spoiler: **Good packaging *does* boost value.** If we engineers are products, our clothes are the packaging, and our employers are the clients paying our salaries. **Would your “client” pay more for a sharper-looking you?** You bet they would!


##### Packaging Sells the Product

It’s no shocker: **well-dressed people command higher pay.** Why? Because **your appearance signals your value.** Just like slick packaging makes a product irresistible, a polished look makes *you* stand out. **Ever notice how a sharp outfit changes how people treat you?**



#### Dress Like a King, Get Treated Like One

**Dress better, get noticed.** Don’t believe me? Ever met an army officer or a top-notch doctor? I live near an army cantonment and saw this firsthand. A young woman (an officer’s daughter) stepped out of a sleek SUV with a sharp-looking jawan. At the dentist, she was treated like royalty—skipped the queue, no questions asked. Meanwhile, I was left waiting.

> Dress like a king, and the world treats you like one.

![](images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/army-officer-treatment.jpg)

We’re superficial creatures, admit it. Think about:
- **Your secret crush**—don’t they usually look good?
- **That head-turner on the street**—weren’t they well-dressed?
- **Your Instagram faves**—aren’t they put-together?

**Dressing well meets people’s expectations of greatness.** If we dress like commoners, we’ll be paid like commoners. **Ready to level up your wardrobe game?**

##### The VIP Effect

That army officer’s daughter? Her sharp vibe screamed “VIP.” **Your clothes communicate status before you say a word.** Want to be taken seriously? **Ditch the sloppy tees and step into something that says, “I’m here to slay.”**


#### What Your Clothes Say About You

Your wardrobe’s shouting something, whether you like it or not. Dress *better* than your job description, and you’re saying, **“I’m more than this role.”** Dress below it? You’re whispering, **“I’m not enough.”**

An engineer who looks sharp despite P1 bugs? They’re saying, **“I value myself, I’m in control.”**
A manager with polished shoes amid chaos? They’re screaming, **“I’ve got this!”**

> Your clothes are a non-verbal megaphone. You can’t *not* communicate—so choose wisely.

Leaders notice these signals. A VP sees a well-dressed engineer and thinks, **“If they nail the small stuff like shoes, they probably nail the big stuff too.”** Guess who’s next in line for a promotion?

##### The Message of Mastery

**Dressing well reflects your inner game.** It says you care about details, you’re deliberate, and you’re ready to lead. **What’s your current outfit saying about you right now?**


#### Why Do we Techies Resist?

We get it—dressing up feels like a chore. **Engineers love coding, not clothes.** We think time spent on outfits steals from our precious problem-solving hours. Fair point, but hear me out.

![Engineering work illustration](/images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/engineering-work-illustration.jpg)

> Engineering is hard work, and we love it—but dressing well doesn’t have to be a buzzkill.

We make time for coffee breaks and meetings (don’t get me started—check my rant on [useless meetings](https://techwiddeep.com/meetings-the-clusterfuck-of-it-industry/)). Why? **They’re habits.** Skip a coffee break or two, and the world won’t end. **Dressing well can be a habit too, with results that compound over time.**

##### The Habit Hack

**Grooming doesn’t have to be a time-suck.** Make it routine, like brushing your teeth. **What’s one small style habit you could start today?** A pressed shirt? A better belt? Small tweaks, big impact.

#### The Social Proof Trap

We look at Mark Zuckerberg’s grey tees or Steve Jobs’ turtlenecks and think, **“They dress down, so why can’t I?”** Here’s the catch: **they’re already legends.** Their success lets them skip the dress code. **We’re not there yet.**

![Zuckerberg Jobs example](/images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/zuckerberg-jobs-example.jpg)

I worked with a guy who rocked crumpled shirts and shabby trousers. People whispered he didn’t “look like a leader.” But he was already successful, so he didn’t care. **For us climbing the ladder, dressing poorly isn’t an option.**

> Don’t misread the social proof—dress for the success you’re chasing.

##### Success First, Sloppiness Later

Zuck and Jobs earned their casual cred. **Until you’re calling the shots, your clothes need to do some talking.** **What’s one outfit you’ve seen that screamed “success” to you?**


This information is widely available, yet we resist dressing better despite the benefits.



#### Avoid the Vanity Trap

Let’s keep it real: **dressing well isn’t about ego—it’s about strategy.** Overdo it, and you’re just preening. I don’t always dress to the nines—**I aim for sharp within reason.** Balance is key.

> Dress well, but don’t fall through the vanity trapdoor.

![Vanity trap illustration](/images/one-techie-to-another-dress-up/vanity-trap-illustration.jpg)


That's my take—now suit yourselves!
