CAP Theorem defines distributed systems' constraints. Introducing the **SQC Theorem** — my take on the same idea for software development. You get to pick 2 out of 3. Never all three.

🔹 **Speed** — how fast you ship
🔹 **Quality** — how solid the product is
🔹 **Cost efficiency** — how lean you operate

Now the combinations:

- Speed + Quality = Cost explodes
- Speed + Cost = Quality dies
- Quality + Cost = Speed tanks

AI raised the baseline. Speed is faster now. The tradeoff? Still there.

Here's what actually happens:

Most startups pick Speed + Cost first. Fast MVP, cheap execution, quality be damned. Market validates, then they scramble — either burn cash for quality or slow down to build it.

**Zerodha** picked Quality + Cost. Small team, selective about what they build, disciplined pace. Dominated Indian broking by saying no to features others chased.

**Infosys/TCS** picked Cost + Scale. Large teams, proven processes, acceptable quality at significantly lower price. Speed? Not their game. Clients accept the tradeoff.

**Tesla early days?** Speed + Cost burn. Move fast, ship bold, iterate constantly. Quality? Panel gaps and production hell. Cost efficiency? Not even a conversation.

**Moral of the story:** Leaders who *mindfully choose* their two variables win. The ones chasing all three end up with mediocre mashup — no clear strength, no real differentiation.

This isn't about being "agile." It's about picking your constraint and owning it completely.
